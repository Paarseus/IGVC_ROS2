## Overview

GNSS receivers find position from ranges to satellites. A standalone receiver uses code (pseudorange) measurements and reaches metre-level accuracy, limited by satellite orbit and clock errors, the ionosphere and troposphere, multipath (reflected signals) and satellite geometry (dilution of precision, DOP). Differential GNSS (DGNSS, including SBAS such as WAAS) cancels much of the shared error using a reference receiver or wide-area corrections. Real-Time Kinematic (RTK) positioning goes further: it uses the carrier phase (the phase of the radio wave, ~19 cm wavelength at L1), which is millimetre-precise but ambiguous by an unknown whole number of cycles. Differencing rover and base-station measurements removes most errors, and the rover then resolves the integer ambiguities; a "FIXED" solution has integers accepted (centimetre level), a "FLOAT" solution still carries them as real numbers (decimetre level). How fast and how reliably the integers are fixed depends on the number and geometry of satellites, the number of frequencies and constellations, signal strength (C/N0, carrier-to-noise density), multipath, the distance to the base (baseline) and the ionosphere; the field's standard method is Teunissen's LAMBDA with validation tests (ratio test, fixed-failure-rate tests) whose failure mode is a wrong ("false") fix. The antenna is often the limiting component: its ground plane, phase-centre behaviour, sky view, nearby metal and electronic noise sources and cabling all change C/N0 and multipath. Corrections come from a single base or a network (VRS, FKP, MAC) and are encoded as RTCM 3 messages (MSM observations, station coordinates, GLONASS biases), usually delivered over the internet with NTRIP (an HTTP-based protocol in which clients may send their NMEA GGA position); clients must detect outages, handle timeouts and reconnect, while receivers degrade as the age of corrections grows. Finally, the antenna position must be referred to the vehicle's reference point through a lever arm (offset vector), both inside a GNSS/INS filter and in ROS (REP-105 frames, navsat_transform's base_link→GPS transform). The standard references are the GNSS textbooks (Springer Handbook of GNSS, Misra & Enge, Kaplan & Hegarty, Groves), the ambiguity-resolution papers of Remondi, Counselman, Hatch and Teunissen, the RTCM 10403 and 10410 (NTRIP) standards, the network-RTK papers, NGS real-time guidelines, IGS antenna-calibration work, and manufacturer documentation (u-blox F9P, Xsens MTi-680G). IMU heading (L2), EKF configuration (L4) and time synchronisation (L5) are excluded.
