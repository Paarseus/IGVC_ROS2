## Overview

Drive kinematics describes how a ground vehicle's body moves (forward speed, sideways speed, turn rate) for given wheel or track speeds. Textbook wheeled-robot kinematics (Campion, Bastin and d'Andréa-Novel's five-type classification; Siegwart and Nourbakhsh; Lynch and Park) assumes each wheel rolls without sliding sideways, which makes a differential drive nonholonomic (it cannot move sideways) and gives the simple inverse `v_L = v − ωB/2`, `v_R = v + ωB/2`. Skid-steer and tracked vehicles break that assumption: they can only turn by scraping their wheels or tracks sideways, so the ground contact shears and slips. Two families of explanation exist. The **kinematic** family (Caracciolo, De Luca and Iannitti 1999; Kozłowski and Pazderski 2004; Martínez et al. 2005; Mandow et al. 2007) says each track acts as if it rolls about its own instantaneous centre of rotation (ICR), and that for a symmetric vehicle this equals a differential drive with a wider "effective" track width plus a longitudinal slip factor — the idea behind the `wheel_separation_multiplier` in ros_controllers / ros2_controllers. The **terramechanics and dynamics** family (Bekker; Janosi and Hanamoto; Steeds; Kitano and Kuma; Ehlert et al.; Wong and Chiang 2001; Wong's *Theory of Ground Vehicles*; Yu et al. 2009/2010) explains *why* the ICRs move: shear stress builds with shear displacement, so turning resistance, slip and therefore the effective width depend on terrain, turn radius, speed, acceleration, load and centre-of-mass position. Parameters are identified offline from spins, arcs and straight runs against an external reference (RTK GNSS, laser, motion capture, lidar), or online with filters and factor graphs (Pentzer et al. 2014; Seegmiller et al. 2013; Zuo et al. 2019; Okawara et al. 2025). Field, agricultural and planetary robotics add slip learning and slip-compensated path following. Encoder odometry error budgets (L1) and motor control (C1) are out of scope.
