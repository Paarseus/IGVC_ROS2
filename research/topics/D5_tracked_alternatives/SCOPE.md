# D5 — Tracked drive alternatives and fixes: scope map

Mapped: 2026-10-06 (step 1 of STANDARDS.md §5). Nothing downloaded in this step; README.md not edited; `sources/` created empty.

## Overview

A tracked skid-steer vehicle turns by driving its two continuous tracks at different speeds, scrubbing the long rigid track-ground contact patch sideways in a way a single wheel's small contact patch does not; the standard account of how this motion is modelled, corrected and calibrated — the instantaneous-centre-of-rotation (ICR) / "effective track width" family descending from Mandow et al.'s 2007 IROS paper, its tracked-specific origin in Martínez et al. 2005, the terramechanics explanation of *why* it happens (Bekker; Janosi & Hanamoto; Wong & Chiang 2001; Wong's *Theory of Ground Vehicles*), and the offline/online calibration literature (Pentzer 2014; Seegmiller 2013; Zuo 2019; Okawara 2025; Focchi 2026; Çiloğlu 2025; Liu 2026) — is already researched in depth in this project's `C2_drive_kinematics` topic, which treats tracked vehicles as one of its two comparator classes alongside wheeled skid-steer. D5 does not re-derive that theory; it cross-references C2 throughout and instead covers what C2's own scope explicitly leaves thin or out: whether track TENSION (not just surface, radius and speed) measurably changes the correction, since C2's own gap check flagged tension and footprint-ratio effects as missing; what techniques beyond a hand-fitted constant multiplier the wider literature recommends (terramechanics-based prediction, online estimators, terrain classification) and whether anything new surfaces for tracks specifically; what tracked-chassis products and kits exist at a competition-robot scale beyond AndyMark's Raptor, and how they integrate their drivetrain; what IGVC teams' and field-robotics (agricultural, orchard, forestry, military) design reports and papers say about a tracked platform's real-world precision and maintenance burden on soft terrain; general wheeled-vs-tracked mobility survey literature (Bruzzone & Quaglia 2012's wheeled/tracked/legged comparison, and Bruzzone, Nodehi & Fanghella's 2022 review specifically of tracked ground-robot locomotion systems, which proposes a body-architecture/track-profile/track-type taxonomy); how maturely a tracked base is supported by the standard ROS 2 / Nav2 stack compared with the `diff_drive_controller`-based support the wheeled-skid-steer topic (D2) found; and a closing net-assessment synthesis of when sources say tracks remain the right choice versus when they recommend switching away. Early searching surfaced genuinely new, not-yet-downloaded leads for most of this — Korean Society of Agricultural Machinery soil-bin studies that vary track tension directly and measure its effect on sinkage and tractive efficiency; patents and a manufacturer application note on track-tension sensing; a 2026 International Journal of Agricultural and Biological Engineering review of slip-ratio control methods for wheeled/tracked harvesters; IGVC design reports describing a team's move away from a tracked platform and another team's tracked entry built on the US Army's GVR-Bot research chassis; and official rubber-track OEM maintenance manuals — while a few other leads (forum threads on competition tank-tread reliability, construction-equipment trade-blog comparisons) surfaced real, live questions but will not clear the source-acceptance checklist and are logged only as unverified context to resolve against better sources later.

**Note on overlap.** Mandow 2007, Martínez 2005, Wong & Chiang 2001, Wong's textbook, Bekker/Janosi & Hanamoto, and Pentzer 2014 are also foundational to the existing `C2_drive_kinematics` topic, which already treats the ICR/effective-width kinematic theory (for both wheeled and tracked skid-steer) and its offline/online calibration in depth, including several sources D5's subtopics 1 and 3 would otherwise duplicate (Dixit & Burdick, Focchi 2026, Çiloğlu 2025, Liu 2026, Seegmiller 2013, Zuo 2019, Okawara 2025, Chamorro 2025). Bruzzone & Quaglia 2012 is similarly shared with sibling topic D2 (wheeled skid-steer), which already cites it as a general locomotion-comparison survey; D5 should draw on the same source without re-fetching it if D2's copy is reusable.
