- **1 header file** (`nav2navigation2_2026_mppi_motion_models.hpp`): reports as `C++ source, ASCII text`, matching its `.hpp` extension.
- **No file's type mismatches its extension**, and no file is an error, login, or bot-check page — every file was individually read or rendered (see table above) and contains the real, on-topic content its citation claims.

Two format-level observations, neither a failure:
1. **`file`'s reported PDF page count is unreliable for several multi-page PDFs** and should not be used as a stand-in for `pdfinfo`/`pdftotext`. Confirmed mismatches: `du_2026_slip_ratio_control_review.pdf` (`file` says 3 pages, `pdfinfo` says 11 — matches the cited 20–30 range), `kayacan_2018_tracked_field_robot_traction.pdf` (`file` says 2, `pdfinfo` says 20), `janwani_2024_gvrbot_safe_hri_cbf.pdf` (`file` says 3, `pdfinfo` says 7). All three were re-verified against `pdfinfo` and their own text; the underlying files are complete and correct, this is purely a quirk of how `file(1)` samples a PDF's page tree.
2. **Seven PDFs have no extractable text layer** (pure image scans, zero or near-zero `pdftotext` output): the three Korean soil-bin/tension papers (`kim_1992…`, `huh_2000…`, `suh_2003…`) and all four U.S. patents (`us11939012_2024…`, `goodyear_2004…`, `deere_2021…`, `caterpillar_2014…`). Each was individually confirmed as real, complete, on-topic content by rendering pages to images (see table). This is not a checklist failure (STANDARDS.md does not require OCR), but it means these seven files cannot be grep/text-verified in a future pass — only visually — which is worth knowing before the next verification step.
