# L2 — IMU and heading: scope map

Mapped: 2026-09-27 (step 1 of STANDARDS.md §5). Nothing downloaded in this step.

## Overview

Heading (the direction a vehicle points, relative to north) is the hardest part of orientation to estimate. Roll and pitch can be found from gravity measured by accelerometers, but gravity carries no information about rotation around the vertical axis, so heading needs an extra reference. The field offers five main references: the Earth's magnetic field (magnetometer, disturbed by nearby iron and by motor currents, so it must be calibrated or mapped), the Earth's rotation (gyrocompassing, which needs gyros far better than typical MEMS parts), GNSS (either the direction of travel from one antenna, which is only valid while the vehicle moves and accelerates, or the vector between two antennas), vision or lidar (which only give heading relative to a start point unless a map is used), and vehicle-motion constraints (a wheeled vehicle does not slide sideways, so the direction of velocity equals heading). Between updates, heading is carried forward by integrating the gyroscope, so gyro error characteristics (bias, bias instability, angle random walk, measured with Allan variance) set how fast heading drifts and what noise values a filter should use. Special motion cases — standstill, slow driving, reverse driving, skidding — are where heading estimates most often fail, and are handled with zero-velocity / zero-rotation updates, motion detection and filter resets. The standard references are the navigation textbooks (Groves, Titterton & Weston, Farrell), the Allan-variance standard IEEE Std 952 and its application papers, the magnetometer-calibration literature (Gebre-Egziabher, Vasconcelos, Kok), land-vehicle aiding papers (Dissanayake et al.), ROS conventions (REP-103/145) and manufacturer documentation (Xsens, VectorNav, SBG, NovAtel). General sensor-fusion configuration (L4), GNSS positioning and RTK (L3) and wheel odometry (L1) are excluded.
