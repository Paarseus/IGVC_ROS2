# C1 — Motor velocity control: scope map

**Mapped:** 2026-09-28 (step 1, map; written after the README already existed, so it doubles as a coverage check)

## Overview
Motor velocity control is the lowest loop in a vehicle's motion stack: a controller turns a commanded wheel or motor speed into a voltage (duty cycle) or current, from a model-based feedforward plus a feedback correction (usually PI, sometimes PID) on a measured speed. The field draws on four bodies of work: (1) general feedback theory: cascaded loops and bandwidth separation, two-degree-of-freedom structure, and limits set by delay, sampling and noise (Åström & Murray; Franklin, Powell & Workman); (2) PID practice: tuning rules (Ziegler–Nichols, relay autotuning, Skogestad SIMC, Kessler's symmetrical optimum from electrical drives), saturation and anti-windup (Fertik & Ross, Hanus, back-calculation); (3) modelling and identification of DC/brushless drives and powertrains (first-order-plus-delay models, Coulomb/viscous/Stribeck friction, LuGre dynamic friction, prediction-error identification after Ljung) together with velocity estimation from quantised encoders and Hall sensors; and (4) practice: how smart motor controllers (REV SPARK MAX, CTRE, maxon/Kollmorgen-type servo drives) expose gains, filters, current and thermal limits, bus-voltage compensation and ramps, and how rovers, AGVs, agricultural and tracked field robots run and report wheel or track speed control. Product-specific material (SPARK MAX firmware 25/26 units, NEO Hall-sensor filtering, WPILib SysId) is one subtopic, placed last.
