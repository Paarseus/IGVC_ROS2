# X2 — Test and validation methods: scope map

**Mapped:** 2026-09-28 (step 1, map; `sources/` was empty at mapping time)

## Overview
Testing a mobile robot's motion and localization stack draws on four bodies of work. (1) **Metrology and statistics**: how to state what was measured and how sure we are of it — accuracy vs precision, repeatability vs reproducibility, measurement uncertainty (JCGM 100 "GUM"), design of experiments, sample size and confidence intervals (NIST/SEMATECH e-Handbook). (2) **Subsystem characterisation tests**: step, ramp and frequency-response tests of velocity loops with time-domain metrics (rise time, overshoot, settling time, steady-state error, integrated error) and frequency-domain metrics (bandwidth, margins) from control textbooks (Åström & Murray); odometry and kinematic benchmarks such as Borenstein & Feng's UMBmark bidirectional square and later calibration-path methods (Kelly). (3) **Localization and trajectory evaluation against ground truth**: reference systems (static/dynamic RTK GNSS, total station/theodolite, laser tracker, motion capture), trajectory alignment (Horn, Umeyama), and error metrics — absolute trajectory error (ATE) and relative pose error (RPE) from the TUM RGB-D benchmark (Sturm et al.), KITTI's per-segment drift metrics (Geiger et al.), relation-based SLAM error (Kümmerle et al.) and the tutorial synthesis of Zhang & Scaramuzza, with tools such as `evo` and `rpg_trajectory_evaluation`. (4) **Standardised performance test methods** for vehicles: NIST/ASTM E54.09 response-robot test methods, ASTM F45 test methods for driverless industrial vehicles (e.g. F3244 navigation in a defined area) with NIST's AGV ground-truth work (Bostelman et al.), ISO 18646-1/-2 (service-robot locomotion and navigation: pose accuracy/repeatability, path deviation, obstacle avoidance), ISO 9283 (manipulator pose/path accuracy — the origin of the accuracy/repeatability definitions reused by mobile-robot standards), ISO 12188-2 (pass-to-pass cross-track error of GNSS auto-guided tractors) and ISO 17123-8 (field test of GNSS RTK precision). Navigation-level metrics (success rate, cross-track error, time to goal, clearance, smoothness) and Nav2's own benchmarking scripts complete the picture; campaign design and reporting (reproducibility guidelines, Bonsignorio et al.) close it. ROS 2/Nav2-specific tooling is one subtopic, placed last.
