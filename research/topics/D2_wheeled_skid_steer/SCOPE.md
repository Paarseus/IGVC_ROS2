# D2 — Wheeled skid-steer / differential drive: scope map

Mapped: 2026-10-06 (step 1 of STANDARDS.md §5). Nothing downloaded in this step; README.md not edited; `sources/` created empty.

## Overview

A wheeled differential-drive (diff-drive) or skid-steer robot steers by commanding the left- and right-side wheels to different speeds rather than turning a wheel's steering axis. For an *ideal* diff-drive — two wheels rolling without lateral slip — this gives the simple, exact textbook kinematics in Campion, Bastin and d'Andréa-Novel's wheeled-mobile-robot classification, Siegwart and Nourbakhsh, and Lynch and Park: `v_L = v − ωB/2`, `v_R = v + ωB/2`, with pure rolling and no-sideslip constraints making the system nonholonomic. A wheeled *skid-steer* robot (3+ wheels per side, or 4/6 wheels with no separate steering axis) must additionally scrub its wheels sideways to turn, which breaks the no-sideslip assumption; Mandow et al.'s 2007 IROS paper is the seminal experimental treatment, modelling each side's instantaneous centre of rotation (ICR) and showing the vehicle behaves like a wider, slower-turning ideal diff-drive — the origin of the "effective track width" / `wheel_separation_multiplier` correction that `ros2_controllers`' `diff_drive_controller` exposes today. Because a wheel's ground contact is a small, roughly circular or elliptical patch (versus a track's long, continuous footprint), the turning-resistance and scrub-torque physics differ from tracked skid-steer, and at least one turning-resistance-coefficient model built directly from tracked-vehicle steering theory reports a smaller coefficient for wheeled skid-steer than for tracked vehicles under comparable conditions — a claim this topic traces and tests against other sources. Odometry-error theory for pure (non-skid) diff-drive is itself a mature sub-field, led by Borenstein and Feng's UMBmark calibration method, which separates unequal-wheel-diameter error from wheel-base-uncertainty error; skid-steer calibration (Mandow and follow-ons: Wang et al. 2015's laser-scanner method, Rabiee and Biswas's 2019 friction-based model, and online EKF/factor-graph estimators) extends this to the ICR/effective-width parameters. ROS 2's `diff_drive_controller` natively supports both the ideal and the skid-corrected case through one multiplier parameter, and Clearpath's Husky, Jackal and Warthog ship as ready-made wheeled skid-steer platforms with this correction pre-tuned, in contrast to the custom kinematic layers tracked platforms typically need. Off-the-shelf kits at competition-robotics scale (AndyMark, VEX) and independent builds (IGVC team designs) offer cheaper wheeled skid-steer options. Terrain traction and precision on grass, dirt and gravel are reported unevenly in the literature — mostly for full-size construction skid-steer loaders and a few field-robotics datasets — and this topic collects what exists while flagging where small-robot evidence is thin.

**How each was filled:**
- Gap 1 (caster vs. skid-steer; controller-plugin motion model): both filled with cited findings. The caster-wheel point needed no new source — it is explicit in the already-downloaded D2-S02 (Siegwart & Nourbakhsh), section 3.2.3.3 ("such wheels do not impose any real constraints on the kinematics of a robot chassis") and Fig. 3.13a (a 2-driven-wheel-plus-castor robot captioned simply "Differential Drive Robot", not skid-steer). The controller-plugin point is filled with two new sources: the official `motion_model` parameter documentation (D2-S36) and the `DiffDriveMotionModel::isHolonomic()` source code, pinned at a commit (D2-S37), confirming it returns `false` (non-holonomic, no Y-axis motion).
- Gap 2 (planetary rover / agricultural domains): both filled. Planetary rover: Shamah's 1999 CMU thesis (D2-S34) on the Nomad testbed (built for planetary-exploration-analog field trials) quantifies that skid steering draws about double the power of explicit (point-turn) steering at a point turn, and that individual wheel torque is distributed unevenly (one wheel carrying up to about 52% of total torque) during a skid-steer turn. Agricultural: Botta et al. 2023 (D2-S35), an open-access peer-reviewed paper on Agri.Q, an eight-wheel articulated wheeled skid-steer robot for hilly/mountainous crop monitoring, reporting understeer from lateral wheel slip during turning and a measured front/rear static load split (60 %/40 %, 112 kg front).
- Gap 3 (pricing): partly filled. No current official price is published by Clearpath (confirmed again — product pages use "request a quote"); the one citable figure found is a dated, named-author IEEE Spectrum article (D2-S38, 2014) giving the Jackal's launch-era price as "high four figures to low five figures" USD and "about half the price" of the Husky of that era. This is added with an explicit currency/date caveat; it is not current pricing.
- Gap 4 (second turning-resistance-mechanism source): partly filled.
