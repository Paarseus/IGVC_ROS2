## Overview

This topic looks at how complete, working systems put motor control, odometry, IMU, GNSS/RTK, state estimation and navigation together, rather than at the theory of any one piece (that is C1–C4 and L1–L5). There are four families of reference systems. (1) **IGVC AutoNav teams**: each year's top teams publish a design report on igvc.org (evidence level C). Recent winners are Sooner Competitive Robotics (Oklahoma, 1st in 2021, 2023, 2024 and 2025, with public GitHub code), Cedarville "Ville Robotics", Boise State "Bender", IIT Kharagpur, Project MANAS and NMIMS. Older open codebases include Georgia Tech RoboJackets `igvc-software` (ROS 1, robot_localization EKF plus GPS, with written notes on IMU problems). (2) **Commercial research platforms** that ship a full ROS 2 configuration: Clearpath Husky/Jackal/Warthog through the shared `clearpath_common`/`clearpath_control` packages and one `robot.yaml`, AgileX `ugv_sdk` and `scout_ros2` (CAN protocol, the base publishes `/odom`), and Robotnik Summit XL (`robotnik_base_control` plus a robot_localization EKF). Alongside these are the framework references they build on: ros2_control and `diff_drive_controller`, robot_localization, and Nav2 with its GPS and odometry-smoothing tutorials. (3) **Other outdoor autonomy stacks**: agricultural robots (Thorvald II, BoniRob), planetary rovers (the MER/Perseverance autonomy stacks, JPL CLARAty, the JPL Open Source Rover), DARPA Grand and Urban Challenge vehicles (Stanley, Boss), and Autoware, the most completely documented automotive stack with separate sensing, localization, planning, control and vehicle-interface modules. (4) **Cross-cutting architecture literature**: three-layer architectures (Gat 1998), the robot-architecture chapter of the Springer Handbook (Kortenkamp & Simmons), and the ROS 2 and Nav2 system papers. These frame the questions this topic asks of every stack: how the layers are split, which loop runs at what rate, which sensors feed which filter, where the safety chain (e-stop, watchdogs, timeouts) sits, and what failed in the field. The design-report and vendor-config evidence is mostly level C. The architecture and field-robot papers are level A.
