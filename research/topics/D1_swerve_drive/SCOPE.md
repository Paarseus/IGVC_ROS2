## Overview

Swerve (also called crab) drive gives every wheel its own steering motor and its own drive motor, so the chassis has as many independently commandable module states as wheels (typically four modules, eight motors) to realize a 3-degree-of-freedom planar body twist (vx, vy, ω). In the standard wheeled-mobile-robot classification (Campion, Bastin and d'Andréa-Novel 1996) this makes a swerve base kinematically redundant rather than simply holonomic: each wheel still rolls without sideways slip (unlike mecanum/omni wheels, which use angled passive rollers to slide sideways by design, and unlike skid-steer/tracks, which only turn by scraping), but because there are more independently actuated degrees of freedom than the 3 the body needs, a consistent command requires every wheel's steering angle to point at a single shared instantaneous center of rotation (ICR); if the ICR falls on or very near a module, the required steering rate goes to infinity — the drivetrain's characteristic singularity. The general method for deriving any wheeled robot's kinematics from each wheel's rolling constraint (Muir and Neuman's wheel-Jacobian formalism, 1986/87) covers steerable wheels as one wheel type, but the specific inverse-kinematics algorithm almost every off-the-shelf swerve module and ROS/FRC codebase actually runs traces to a non-academic source: a kinematics derivation first circulated on the FIRST Robotics Competition (FRC) community site Chief Delphi around 2011 (attributed to the user "Ether"), later formalized as WPILib's `SwerveDriveKinematics`/`SwerveDrivePoseEstimator` and extended with "module-angle optimization" and discrete-time skew correction for simultaneous translate+rotate motion. Outside FRC/FTC competition robotics, the same independently-steered-and-driven-wheel structure appears under different names in automotive/AGV literature (4WIS4WID — four-wheel-independent-steering, four-wheel-independent-drive — vehicles) and, loosely, in planetary rover steering-actuator design (Mars Exploration Rover). Published, hobbyist and competition-team material on swerve is extensive for the control and odometry side (closed-loop per-module velocity+position control, Kalman-filter pose estimators, discretization-based skew correction) and for commercial module products (REV, Swerve Drive Specialties, West Coast Products, ThriftyBot, ARMABot and others, all aimed at the FRC/FTC market and its flat-carpet playing field). It is comparatively thin on outdoor use: swerve modules are built and sold for indoor carpet, ROS 2/Nav2 has no official swerve controller in `ros2_controllers` (only Ackermann/bicycle/tricycle single-steered-axle models; swerve support is community code), and no verified outdoor grass/mud/water deployment report or quantitative swerve-vs-skid-steer/Ackermann precision comparison has yet been found. One directly relevant precedent exists: an IGVC AutoNav team (Oakland University, "Swervi," 2022 design report) built and competed with a swerve-drive IGVC robot, and a second (Sooner Competitive Robotics, "Twistopher," 2025) is a recent IGVC entry to check for a drivetrain description.
