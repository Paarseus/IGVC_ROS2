# L4 — Sensor fusion: scope map

Mapped: 2026-09-28 (step 1 of STANDARDS.md §5). Nothing downloaded in this step.

## Overview

Sensor fusion for a mobile robot means combining several imperfect measurements (wheel odometry, IMU, GNSS, visual or lidar odometry) into one best estimate of the robot's pose (position and orientation) and velocity, together with an honest estimate of how uncertain that estimate is. The dominant tool is the Kalman filter family: the linear Kalman filter (Kalman 1960), the extended Kalman filter (EKF, which linearises nonlinear motion and measurement models around the current estimate) and the unscented Kalman filter (UKF, which propagates a small set of sample points instead of linearising; Julier & Uhlmann 1997/2004), with the error-state and invariant EKF as newer variants and factor-graph smoothers (Dellaert & Kaess) as the main alternative. In ROS the conventions for where each estimate lives are fixed by REP-103 (units, axes) and REP-105 (the `map` → `odom` → `base_link` chain: `odom` is continuous but drifts, `map` does not drift but may jump), and the standard implementation is robot_localization (Moore & Stouch 2014), which fuses an arbitrary number of sensors into a 15-state EKF/UKF and, with `navsat_transform_node`, turns GNSS latitude/longitude into the robot's world frame; the maintainers' recommended outdoor setup is two filters (a local one for `odom → base_link`, a global one with GNSS for `map → odom`). Whether such a filter works in practice depends almost entirely on configuration: which variables each sensor feeds (absolute, differential or relative), the covariances reported by drivers or assigned by hand, process noise Q and initial covariance P0, outlier gating by Mahalanobis distance, handling of delayed and out-of-order data, and changes in GNSS quality (RTK fixed, float, standalone). Whether the result is trustworthy is judged with consistency tests from the tracking literature (NEES and NIS chi-square tests; Bar-Shalom, Li & Kirubarajan), and failures on field robots (divergence, overconfidence, frame and sign errors, jumps, drift from bad covariance) are diagnosed from innovations, covariances and TF behaviour. GNSS receiver behaviour (L3), IMU internals (L2), wheel odometry derivation (L1) and time synchronisation (L5) are excluded; this topic takes their outputs as given inputs to the filter.
